# AZ-104 Exam-Day Guide

This page covers the logistics of the Microsoft Azure Administrator (AZ-104) exam: booking, delivery options, check-in, exam format, scoring, renewal, and what happens afterward. Every fact links to the official Microsoft Learn page it came from. Policies change, so confirm anything time-sensitive on the linked page before your appointment.

> Also see the [AZ-104 practice questions](https://www.savemycert.com/practice/azure-administrator-associate/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=az-104-study-guide) and [timed mock exams](https://www.savemycert.com/mocks/azure-administrator-associate/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=az-104-study-guide).

## Booking the exam

On the Azure Administrator Associate certification page, choose **Schedule exam** under Pearson VUE. You sign in to your Learn profile, confirm your legal name (it must match your ID), and add contact details before Pearson VUE takes over. Microsoft strongly recommends a personal Microsoft account (MSA): exam records tied to a work or school account are lost if you leave that organization.

You can book up to 90 days ahead, with at most two Microsoft exams scheduled at once. Reschedule or cancel from your Learn profile at least 24 hours before the appointment, or your fee is forfeited. Microsoft does not refund failed exams or missed appointments.

The price depends on the country or region where you test, and taxes may apply, so check the certification page for your local price. The exam is offered in 10 languages, listed on the same page.

Source: [Azure Administrator Associate certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-administrator/), [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [Reschedule and cancellation policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/reschedule-and-cancellation-policy), [Exam FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/frequently-asked-questions)

## Online (OnVUE) vs test center

In most cases you can test online or at a Pearson VUE test center. A test center is a quiet, pre-configured room with no software to install. Online, you are responsible for your computer and room meeting the security rules:

- Run Pearson VUE's system test on the same computer, network, and location, before booking and again on exam day. Prefer a personal computer, since work machines often block the OnVUE software.
- Use a walled room with a closed door. No one else may enter during the exam.
- Clear the desk of books, notes, and pens, unplug extra monitors, and turn off other computers.
- Keep your phone out of arm's reach but close enough to hear the proctor call. Greeters and proctors communicate in English.

Exams with labs need test centers that meet extra connectivity requirements, so fewer locations may be listed.

Source: [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams), [Exam FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/frequently-asked-questions)

## ID and check-in

You need a current government-issued ID, and the name on it must match the name on your Microsoft Certification profile exactly.

For an online exam, check in from your Learn profile between 30 minutes before and 15 minutes after your appointment. You photograph your face, ID, and exam area with your phone, and a greeter reviews them before placing you in the proctor queue. Check-in takes about 15 minutes.

Source: [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)

## How the exam is structured

You have 100 minutes; Microsoft suggests 120 minutes of seat time for associate exams of that length. Most Microsoft exams have 40 to 60 questions.

Microsoft does not list question types per exam. The page says you "may have interactive components," and Microsoft does not publish which exams contain labs. The introduction screen tells you how many questions, case studies, and labs your exam has. The free [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo) demonstrates the possible types, including case studies and labs.

Once you leave a case study or lab, you cannot go back to it. On associate exams you can browse learn.microsoft.com in a split screen, but no extra time is added.

Five minutes are built in for unscheduled breaks. The clock keeps running during a break, and afterward you cannot return to questions you have already seen.

The skills measured are identities and governance (20–25%), storage (15–20%), compute (20–25%), virtual networking (15–20%), and monitoring and maintenance (10–15%).

Source: [Azure Administrator Associate certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-administrator/), [Exam duration and exam experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience), [Exam FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/frequently-asked-questions), [AZ-104 study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/az-104)

## How scoring works

Scores are reported on a scale of 1 to 1,000, and you need 700 or more to pass. Because the score is scaled, 700 does not necessarily mean 70% of the points.

Multi-part questions usually give one point per correct part, and there is no penalty for guessing. Some questions are unscored and unmarked, so answer everything. Lab tasks are scored on the end result, not the method, so the portal, PowerShell, or the CLI all work.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports), [Exam FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/frequently-asked-questions)

## Results and score report

Most results arrive within minutes; exams with labs take about 30 minutes. The report shows your score, pass or fail, and a bar chart by skill area, but not which questions you missed. Your result appears in your Learn profile within 24 hours.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports)

## Your credential and renewal

When you pass, the certification appears in your Learn profile under Credentials with an Online Verifiable tag. You can share it on LinkedIn or by link and download a certificate. Others can verify it only while your profile is public.

AZ-104 expires after 12 months unless renewed. Renewal is free: pass a short, unproctored, open-book online assessment on Microsoft Learn during your six-month eligibility window. Passing extends the certification one year from its expiration date. Renewal assessment retakes are unlimited.

Source: [Sharing and validating of Microsoft Credentials](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cred-share-validate), [Microsoft Certification Renewal](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification), [Exam retake policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy)

## If you don't pass

- After a first failed attempt, wait 24 hours before retaking.
- After that, wait 14 days between attempts.
- You can take the same exam at most five times in 12 months, counted from your first attempt.
- You pay for each retake.

Source: [Exam retake policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy)

## On exam day: practical tips

- **Read the intro screen.** Budget time from its question, case study, and lab counts.
- **Don't wait on lab tasks.** If a task is still running, move to one that doesn't depend on it.
- **Use Learn sparingly.** Look up a specific setting, not every answer.
- **Answer before a break.** Anything you have seen before a break is locked afterward.
- **Never leave a blank.** A wrong answer costs nothing.
